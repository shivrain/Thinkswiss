# 4P Index coding — Senegal Code de l'Environnement (2001 and 2023)

This folder contains completed **Plastic Pollution Policy Index (4P Index)** coding tables
(Columns A–W, per the *4P Index — Policy Coding Prompt v2*) for Senegal's environmental code,
produced as downloadable artifacts. It now covers **both** the original 2001 law and its 2023
replacement:

| Law | Files | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Loi n° 2001-01 du 15 janvier 2001 | `senegal_loi_2001-01_*` | Repealed, replaced by the 2023 law |
| **Loi n° 2023-15 du 02 août 2023** | `senegal_loi_2023-15_*` | **Current, in-force Code de l'Environnement** |

## Files

- `senegal_loi_2023-15_4p_index.xlsx` / `.csv` — coding of the **current (2023) Code**, the one to use
  for any live/present-day assessment of Senegal's environmental framework. Generated by
  `build_4p_index_2023.py`.
- `senegal_loi_2001-01_4p_index.xlsx` / `.csv` — coding of the **superseded 2001 Code**, kept for
  historical reference/comparison. Generated by `build_4p_index.py`.
- `build_4p_index.py` / `build_4p_index_2023.py` — source-of-truth scripts that generate the CSV/XLSX
  outputs from structured coded data; edit and re-run (e.g. `python3 build_4p_index_2023.py`) to
  regenerate outputs if the coding changes.

## Document verification (2023 law)

The user supplied a PDF (`Loi-no-2023-15-du-02-aou-t-2023-portant-Code-de-lEnvironnement_d106.pdf`).
It is a scanned image PDF (no embedded text layer), so it was OCR'd (`tesseract`, French model) at
300 dpi to confirm its identity. The OCR output matches the official publication: **Journal Officiel
de la République du Sénégal, 88e année, n° 7660, samedi 16 septembre 2023**, containing "*Loi n°
2023-15 du 02 août 2023 portant Code de l'Environnement*", adopted by the National Assembly on 7 June
2023, reviewed by the Constitutional Council (decision n° 5/C/2023 of 12 July 2023), and expressly
repealing Loi n° 2001-01 (its Art. 255: *"La présente loi abroge et remplace la loi n° 2001-01 du 15
janvier 2001 portant Code de l'Environnement."*). **This is confirmed to be the correct/right document
for this coding task.** Given the OCR quality on the scanned upload was noisy, the actual coding below
was performed against a clean full-text mirror of the same law (primature.sn), cross-checked line-for-
line against the OCR of the uploaded PDF for the exposé des motifs and adoption/promulgation clauses.

## What was coded — Loi n° 2023-15 (2023), the current Code

**Policy coded:** *Loi n° 2023-15 du 02 août 2023 portant Code de l'Environnement* (Senegal) — 255
articles across 7 titles, repealing and replacing the 2001 Code (Art. 255).

Primary sources consulted (full text): the user-uploaded PDF (OCR-verified), plus the clean mirrors
https://primature.sn/sites/default/files/2023-10/Loi%20n%C2%B02023-15%20du%2002%20ao%C3%BBt%202023%20portant%20Code%20de%20l%27Environnement.pdf
and https://www.vie-publique.sn/documents/7949/loi-n-2023-15-du-02-aout-2023-portant-code-de-l-environnement-7949.

**Plastics relevance:** like the 2001 Code, the 2023 Code never mentions "plastique" / synthetic
polymers explicitly (confirmed by full-text search). All 12 coded instruments qualify under filter
criteria (b) and/or (c) — they are waste-management/governance instruments or instruments that
directly enable plastic-relevant actions (the codified EPR principle and waste hierarchy, the food-
contact recycled-packaging ban, municipal waste planning, household source-sorting) rather than
plastics-named provisions. Senegal's plastics-specific obligations remain in separate, freestanding
legislation (*Loi n° 2015-09* of 2015, replaced by *Loi n° 2020-04* of 2020), cross-referenced in
Column W.

**What's new/stronger vs. the 2001 Code** (see Column W for article-level detail):
- The **EPR principle** and the **waste hierarchy** (reuse > recycling > energy recovery > disposal)
  are now explicit, codified general principles (Art. 5, Art. 69) — the clearest general-law hook for
  plastics circularity.
- **Two ring-fenced funds** instead of one: the general Environmental Protection Fund (Art. 18) plus a
  new dedicated **marine/coastal fund (FN-PEM, Art. 19)** — directly relevant to marine plastic litter.
- A new **mandatory environmental-audit regime** (Art. 42-43) for ongoing operations, on top of the
  pre-project EIA regime.
- A new **mandatory household source-sorting duty** (Art. 89, "tri à la source") and a new **mandatory
  municipal waste-management planning obligation** (Art. 87) — neither existed in the 2001 Code.
- A new **criminal ban on using recycled waste-derived materials in food-contact packaging
  manufacture** (Art. 210) — a food-safety restriction that specifically constrains recycled-plastic
  packaging, flagged as a notable tension with the Code's own circular-economy principles.
- The old Art. L40 "waste-generating products" **enabling power** (the 2001 Code's contingent hook for
  banning items like single-use plastics, coded as *not in force*, V=0, in the 2001 table) has **no
  direct equivalent** in the 2023 Code — this role now sits with the freestanding plastics law (Loi n°
  2020-04) and the newly-binding EPR/waste-hierarchy duties (Art. 5, 65-71), which is why every
  instrument in the 2023 table is coded as **in force** (S=1).

**Summary of results (2023 Code):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (1 + 1 + 1 + 1) / 4 = **1.0** (legislation; 15 sectors ⇒ 7+
  bracket; all 5 life-cycle phases; two ring-fenced funds).
- **12 instrument rows** coded, spanning ICPE permitting, financial instruments/funds, EIA, the new
  environmental-audit regime, the EPR/waste-hierarchy duty, the open-burning ban, absolute dumping/
  hazardous-waste-import bans, municipal waste planning, household source-sorting, the recycled-
  packaging ban, State–municipality institutional coordination, and emergency response.
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.45** (municipal waste planning; institutional
  coordination) to **0.875** (ICPE permitting; EPR/waste-hierarchy duty; absolute dumping bans).
  Unlike the 2001 table, no instrument scores 0 here — see the "what's new" note above.

## What was coded — Loi n° 2001-01 (2001, superseded)

**Policy coded:** *Loi n° 2001-01 du 15 janvier 2001 portant Code de l'Environnement* (Senegal).

Primary sources consulted (full text):
- https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/sen34608.pdf
- https://acbep.gouv.sn/sites/default/files/2021-12/Loi%20n%C2%B0%202001-01%20du%2012%20avril%202001%20portant%20code%20de%20l%27environnement.pdf
- https://www.sante.gouv.sn/sites/default/files/2.%2001-01-15CODENVIRONLOI.pdf
- Décret n° 2001-282 portant application du Code de l'environnement (implementing decree), for context.

**Date discrepancy note:** the task brief cited "*Loi n° 2001-01 du 12 avril 2001*". All three primary
legislative texts located show the law was deliberated by the National Assembly (29 Dec 2000) and the
Senate (4 Jan 2001) and is dated **15 January 2001**. 12 April 2001 is the date of the separate
*Décret n° 2001-282* (implementing decree for classified installations, EIA procedure, water/air/noise
pollution — issued under this law). This is flagged in Column W of the first row and in the policy
title (Column A) uses the law's own dated title.

**Supersession note:** Loi n° 2001-01 was repealed and replaced by *Loi n° 2023-15 du 2 août 2023
portant Code de l'Environnement*, adopted 7 June 2023. Per the coding rules, this table codes the 2001
law as the specific instrument requested (year = 2001, its own enactment year), since the 2023 law is a
distinct, separately-numbered replacement rather than an amendment of the 2001 text. A separate 4P Index
entry should be created for Loi n° 2023-15 if the current legal framework needs to be assessed.

**Plastics relevance:** the 2001 Code never mentions "plastique" / synthetic polymers explicitly. All
eight coded instruments qualify under filter criteria (b) and/or (c) of the coding prompt — they are
waste-management/governance instruments (classified-installations permitting, waste elimination duty,
dumping/hazardous-waste-import bans, EIA, emergency planning, State–municipality coordination) or an
enabling power (Art. L40) that directly enables future plastic-specific bans — rather than because the
Code names plastics. Senegal later exercised plastics-specific policy powers through separate,
freestanding legislation (*Loi n° 2015-09* of 4 May 2015, replaced by *Loi n° 2020-04* of 8 January
2020), which are cross-referenced in the comments column (W) and should be coded as their own policy
entries in the 4P Index rather than folded into this one.

**Summary of results (2001 Code):**

- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (G + I + K + M) / 4 = (1 + 1 + 1 + 1) / 4 = **1.0**
  (Legislation; 13 sectors touched ⇒ 7+ bracket; all 5 life-cycle phases addressed in general terms;
  ring-fenced Environmental Protection Fund financed by the Code's own pollution levies).
- **8 instrument rows** were coded (Columns P–W), covering: classified-installations permitting,
  pollution taxes/Environmental Protection Fund, general waste-elimination duty, absolute
  dumping/hazardous-waste-import bans, the Art. L40 waste-generating-products enabling power (not yet in
  force under this law), mandatory EIA, emergency/internal-operations planning, and the
  State–municipality coordination framework.
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.0** (Art. L40 enabling power, not yet operationalised) to
  **1.0** (absolute dumping/hazardous-waste-import bans).
