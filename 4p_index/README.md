# 4P Index coding — Senegal environmental regulatory framework (2001–2023)

(Columns A–W, per the *4P Index — Policy Coding Prompt v2*) for Senegal's environmental code and its
implementing instruments, produced as downloadable artifacts. It now covers **fourteen** documents:

## Document verification (2023 law)

The user supplied a PDF (`Loi-no-2023-15-du-02-aou-t-2023-portant-Code-de-lEnvironnement_d106.pdf`).
It is a scanned image PDF (no embedded text layer), so it was OCR'd (`tesseract`, French model) at
300 dpi to confirm its identity. The OCR output matches the official publication: **Journal Officiel
de la République du Sénégal, 88e année, n° 7660, samedi 16 septembre 2023**, containing "*Loi n°
2023-15 du 02 août 2023 portant Code de l'Environnement*", adopted by the National Assembly on 7 June
2023, reviewed by the Constitutional Council (decision n° 5/C/2023 of 12 July 2023), and expressly
repealing Loi n° 2001-01 (its Art. 255: *"La présente loi abroge et remplace la loi n° 2001-01 du 15
janvier 2001 portant Code de l'Environnement."*). **This is confirmed to be the correct/right document
for this coding task.** Given the OCR quality on the scanned upload was noisy, the actual coding below
was performed against a clean full-text mirror of the same law (primature.sn), cross-checked line-for-
line against the OCR of the uploaded PDF for the exposé des motifs and adoption/promulgation clauses.

## What was coded — Décret n° 2001-282 (2001), the implementing decree

**Policy coded:** *Décret n° 2001-282 du 12 avril 2001 portant application du Code de l'Environnement*
— 86 articles across 6 titles (classified installations; EIA; water pollution; water policing; air
pollution; noise pollution), signed at Dakar on 12 April 2001 by President Abdoulaye Wade and PM
Mame Madior Boye. Source: user-uploaded PDF (clean, machine-readable text, confirmed to match
https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/sen37192.pdf as supplied in the task brief) — **confirmed correct**.

**Date-discrepancy resolution:** earlier task briefs referenced "*the Code de l'Environnement...du 12
avril 2001*", which did not match either the 2001 Law (dated 15 January 2001) or the 2023 Law (dated
2 August 2023). Coding this document resolves that discrepancy: **12 April 2001 is this decree's own
date**, not the Law's — the original source database entry evidently conflated the Law's Journal
Officiel citation with its implementing decree's date.

**Plastics relevance:** like both laws, this decree never mentions "plastique" (confirmed by full-text
search). All 7 coded instruments qualify under filter criteria (b)/(c) — they operationalise the ICPE
permitting regime, EIA procedure, water-discharge standards, pollution taxes, marine/coastal dumping
prohibitions, and air-emission/incineration-equipment standards that plastics production, effluent and
waste-incineration activities are subject to in practice. The noise-pollution title (Titre VI, Art.
R83-R86) was reviewed but **excluded** from the coding table as it does not meet any of the three
Plastics Relevance Filter criteria.

**Policy-type note:** this is an executive/presidential decree (issued after Conseil d'Etat review, not
enacted by Parliament), so it is coded 0.75 rather than 1 — a different Column G value from the parent
laws it implements, per Coding Rule 6.

**Summary of results (2001 Decree):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (G + I + K + M) / 4 = (0.75 + 1 + 0.75 + 0.5) / 4 = **0.75**
  (executive decree; 14 sectors ⇒ 7+ bracket; 4 of 5 life-cycle phases addressed — production,
  recycling, disposal, environmental leakage, but not consumption; taxes/funding mentioned but the
  ring-fencing declaration itself lives in the parent law, not this decree, so M = 0.5 rather than 1).
- **7 instrument rows** coded: the ICPE authorisation/declaration procedure, the EIA procedure, water
  effluent discharge standards, the pollution-tax computation mechanism, marine/coastal water-police
  prohibitions and enforcement, fixed-installation air-pollution control and special protection zones,
  and incineration/combustion-equipment technical standards and periodic inspection.
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.45** (EIA procedure) to **0.875** (ICPE regime; marine/
  coastal dumping prohibitions; air-pollution control; incineration-equipment standards). Every
  instrument in this decree is coded **in force** (S=1); no purely-contingent, unexercised enabling
  power was identified as its own separate instrument.

## What was coded — Norme NS 05-061 (2001), wastewater discharge standard

**Policy coded:** *Norme sénégalaise NS 05-061 — Eaux usées : Normes de rejet* (July 2001), adopted by
the ISN's environment technical committee (ISN/CT5), together with the accompanying **Arrêté
Interministériel fixant les conditions de rejets des eaux usées** (signed jointly by the Ministers of
Youth/Environment/Public Hygiene and of Handicrafts/Industry; undated in the extracted text but citing
decrees up to 30 August 2001) that makes the standard legally binding. Source: user-uploaded PDF
(Source: Direction de l'Environnement et des Etablissements Classés / Institut Sénégalais de
Normalisation), consistent with the task-brief URL.

**Relationship to the other coded documents:** the standard's own Avant-Propos states it "vient
compléter le décret n° 2001-282 du 12 avril 2001" — i.e. it is the detailed technical annex to the
implementing decree already coded in this series, and Art. 7 of its implementing Arrêté cross-references
penal sanctions in both Loi n° 2001-01 (Art. L96-L98, L100) and Décret n° 2001-282.

**Policy-type note:** the underlying document is a *technical standard*, not itself a legal instrument —
but the joint ministerial Arrêté that operationalises it (mandatory 6-month compliance deadline, express
sanctions, mandatory annual pollution charge) elevates it to a binding sub-legislative regulation, coded
0.75 like the implementing decree, per Coding Rule 6.

**Plastics relevance:** the standard never mentions "plastique" explicitly (confirmed by full-text
search). All 5 coded instruments qualify under filter criterion (c) — they are the technical
discharge-standard, monitoring and pollution-charge instruments that plastics-sector industrial effluent
(e.g. from manufacturing, washing, extrusion or recycling operations) is subject to in practice.

**Summary of results (Norme NS 05-061):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (0.75 + 1 + 0.75 + 0.5) / 4 = **0.75** (binding technical
  standard/ministerial order; 10 sectors ⇒ 7+ bracket; 4 of 5 life-cycle phases — production,
  consumption, disposal, environmental leakage, but not recycling; mandatory pollution charge mentioned
  but not itself declared ring-fenced within this document, so M = 0.5).
- **5 instrument rows** coded: effluent quality/discharge limit values, absolute discharge prohibitions,
  the monitoring/sampling/accreditation regime, the pollution-charge (redevance) mechanism, and the
  enhanced protection-zone regime for sensitive water bodies (e.g. Baie de Hann).
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.575** (monitoring/accreditation regime) to **1.0**
  (absolute discharge prohibitions). Every instrument is coded in force (S=1); Column W flags one
  genuine borderline case (the general case-by-case zero-discharge negotiation track for existing
  dischargers into multi-use water bodies uses softer, more contingent language than the flagship
  Baie-de-Hann example).

## What was coded — Loi n° 2020-04, the flagship plastics law

**Policy coded:** *Loi n° 2020-04 du 8 janvier 2020 relative à la prévention et à la réduction de
l'incidence sur l'environnement des produits plastiques* — Senegal's dedicated, comprehensive plastics
law, adopted by the National Assembly on 30 December 2019 and signed by President Macky Sall on 8
January 2020. Fetched directly from the internet (FAOLEX mirror, cross-consistent with both
task-brief URLs); confirmed authentic and complete (42 articles across 10 chapters). Per its own
Art. 40, it **repeals and replaces Loi n° 2015-09** (the earlier, narrower thin-bag ban), consistent
with the task brief.

**Why this document differs from every other in this series:** every other Senegalese instrument
coded so far is a general environmental/sanitation/local-government/fisheries law in which plastics
appear only incidentally (if at all). This law is different — it is *entirely and exclusively* about
plastic products and plastic waste, from its title through all 42 articles. Accordingly, nearly every
provision passes the Plastics Relevance Filter under criterion (a) directly (explicit mention of
plastics/synthetic-polymer products), making this the richest, most heavily-instrumented table in the
series.

**Summary of results (Loi n° 2020-04):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (1 + 1 + 1 + 0.5) / 4 = **0.875** (legislation; 14 sectors ⇒
  7+ bracket; **all 5 life-cycle phases** addressed — production, consumption, recycling, disposal,
  environmental leakage; the law creates pricing/tax/security-deposit mechanisms but does not itself
  ring-fence tax proceeds into a dedicated fund, so M = 0.5).
- **Policy target (Column E)** = 0.5: Art. 16 explicitly mentions a recycled-content-integration
  target for new plastic products, but defers the actual quantified percentage/deadline to a future
  decree, so it is coded as a non-quantifiable plastic-specific target rather than 1.
- **13 instrument rows** coded, covering nearly the full task-brief description verbatim: the single-
  use/disposable plastics ban (Art. 4), the checkout-bag ban (Art. 5), the bottle deposit-refund
  scheme (Art. 6-7), producer bottle collection/valorisation/reporting duties (Art. 8-10), the EPR
  regime — individual programmes and eco-organismes (Art. 11-14), the waste-reduction/eco-design duty
  (Art. 15), the recycled-content mandate (Art. 16), mandatory product marking (Art. 17), the
  consumer take-back duty (Art. 18), the plastic-waste import ban (Art. 19), the plastic-waste export-
  authorisation regime (Art. 20), the recycled-plastic floor price (Art. 21), and the "taxe
  plastique" on non-recyclable plastic products (Art. 22).
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.0** (the plastic tax, Art. 22 — a genuine Rule 12
  "not yet operationalised" case: the tax has no operative content — no taxed-product list, rate or
  collection procedure — without its still-pending implementing decree) to **1.0** (the single-use
  plastics ban, Art. 4, and the bottle collection/take-back duty, Art. 8-10 — both fully evidenced
  across all four implementation sub-criteria).
- One notable implementation gap flagged in Column W: the waste-reduction/eco-design duty (Art. 15)
  is the only substantive producer obligation in the law's core chapters without its own dedicated
  criminal penalty among Art. 26-39, unlike every comparable duty.

## What was coded — Plan de Gestion des Déchets Biomédicaux (Mai 2019, national plan)

**Policy coded:** *Réactualisation Plan de Gestion des Déchets Biomédicaux*, Mai 2019 — a national
sectoral plan prepared by the Ministère de la Santé et de l'Action Sociale (MSAS) with World Bank
technical/financial support under the REDISSE and ISMEA health-system projects (originally elaborated
in March 2014, revised 2016, updated again in May 2019). Fetched directly from the task-brief URL
(sante.gouv.sn); a 77-page, 1957-line document.

**Key finding — extensive, explicit plastics content:** unlike most documents in this series, this
plan discusses plastics repeatedly and explicitly throughout: **colour-coded plastic bags and
containers are the prescribed packaging medium for every biomedical-waste category** ("sachets
plastiques noirs/jaunes", "sacs en plastique", "boîtes de sécurité en plastique"); plastic bottles/
packaging are explicitly listed within the general/household waste stream; and the technical-guidance
section on elimination technologies explicitly identifies **"Plastiques Halogénés (PVC)"** as a waste
category unsuitable for incineration (due to toxic dioxin emissions), directing such waste toward
alternative treatment. This satisfies Plastics Relevance Filter criterion (a) directly for several
instruments, not merely the general waste-governance nexus of criterion (c).

**Policy-type note:** as a national plan/programme (not a law or decree) with a quantified, costed
and time-bound action matrix and a specific 1.6 billion FCFA budget, it is coded 0.50 rather than
0.25 (aspirational-only) per the Column G scale.

**Summary of results (Plan DBM 2019):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (0.50 + 1 + 0.75 + 1) / 4 = **0.812** (plan with quantifiable
  action-cost targets; 8 sectors ⇒ 7+ bracket; 3 of 5 life-cycle phases — consumption, disposal,
  environmental leakage; a specific, fully-dedicated budget of 1.6 billion FCFA, ring-fenced for this
  plan's own actions).
- **7 instrument rows** coded: the colour-coded plastic segregation/packaging standard, the
  incinerator/treatment-infrastructure investment programme (with private-sector cost-sharing), a
  proposed (not-yet-enacted) polluter-pays/EPR regulatory reform for private waste producers,
  advocacy for dedicated facility-level DBM budget lines, technical guidance excluding PVC/
  halogenated plastics from incineration, the training/awareness strategy, and the multi-sectoral
  institutional-coordination and monitoring-evaluation framework.
- Instrument scores (Column V) range from **0.0** (the proposed polluter-pays/EPR reform — a genuine
  Rule 12 "not yet exercised" case, explicitly framed in the plan as a future recommendation rather
  than an operative rule) to **0.75** (the plastic-packaging segregation standard).

## What was coded — Décret n° 2016-1804, implementing decree for the Fisheries Code

**Policy coded:** *Décret n° 2016-1804 du 22 novembre 2016 portant application de la loi n° 2015-18
du 13 juillet 2015 portant Code de la Pêche maritime*, which repeals and replaces the prior
implementing decree (Décret n° 98-498 du 10 juin 1998). Source: user-uploaded PDF (clean,
machine-readable text, hosted on the droit-afrique.com legal-text mirror), consistent with the
task-brief URL.

**Key finding — thinnest plastics relevance in this series:** an exhaustive full-text search for
"plastique", "nylon", "monofilament", "déchet", "immersion", "rejet", "abandon", "épave", "détritus",
"résidu", "ordure" and "pollution" returned **no matches anywhere** in this 26-page decree. Critically,
unlike the parent law it implements (Loi n° 2015-18, Art. 66, which explicitly bans nylon monofilament/
multimonofilament gillnets — already coded as its own policy entry in this series), **this
implementing decree does not itself repeat, extend or further operationalise that plastics-relevant
gear ban** — the ban remains entirely self-executing directly from the Code's own text, with no
elaborating detail added here. The decree's substantive content (mesh-size tables, gear/vessel-
tonnage bans for conservation purposes, minimum catch sizes for ~40 species, precise geographic
fishing-zone coordinates, licensing procedures, vessel marking specifications, and the observer
programme) is overwhelmingly about fish-stock conservation and fisheries administration, not plastics
or waste governance.

**Coding approach and honesty note:** given this genuine scarcity of plastics-relevant content, only
two provisions bearing an indirect, borderline relationship to gear-based marine governance were
coded, each with an explicit Rule 8 uncertainty flag rather than being force-fitted as clearly
plastics-relevant. This is intentionally the most conservative coding table in the series, reflecting
an honest application of the Plastics Relevance Filter to a document that is, on its own terms,
almost entirely about fisheries-stock management rather than plastics or waste.

**Summary of results (Décret n° 2016-1804):**
- **Policy-level score (Column O)** = (0.75 + 1 + 0.25 + 0) / 4 = **0.50** (implementing decree; 10
  sectors ⇒ 7+ bracket via its wide range of executing/advisory ministries; only 1 of 5 life-cycle
  phases clearly implicated — environmental leakage, via general habitat/marine-environment
  protection language; no budget/funding mechanism mentioned).
- **2 instrument rows** coded: the national/local fisheries-governance coordination bodies (Conseil
